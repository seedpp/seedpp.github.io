Aim of the project

Six things you will know how to do by the end.

The aim of this training project is to provide academic support on specific topics with a prospective outcome: a paper. Each stage of the coaching maps to one skill you keep for the rest of your career.

01

Do a literature review paper

Choose a review method that fits your question and scope it so it can actually be finished.

02

Search and analyse papers

Build a search strategy, screen what you find, and read at the level a synthesis needs.

03

Write an academic review paper

Structure the argument, report the method transparently, and write in publishable English.

04

Find an appropriate journal

Match scope, audience and indexing — and read a journal's aims before you commit to it.

05

Submit a paper

Handle the submission system, the cover letter and every file a journal asks for.

06

Respond to editors and reviewers

Read a decision letter calmly, and answer each comment so the revision is easy to accept.

01About the papers
  • Topics: discussed and agreed with the supervising team.
  • Type: a review paper.
  • Method: a literature review — metasynthesis, scoping review, systematic review, narrative review and similar. If you are in the Social Sciences, a bibliometric review is excluded.
  • Word count: approximately 6,000 words, excluding references.
02Benefits for awardees
  • Authorship: you are first author and corresponding author if you are the only awardee on your topic. With more than two awardees, authorship is agreed among the awardees.
  • Learn how to write an academic paper, and specifically a literature review paper.
  • Learn how to find an appropriate journal.
  • Learn how to respond to reviewers if the paper moves forward.
  • Build up your academic résumé.
  • Please note: you do not receive a salary from ATS, and you do not pay ATS.
03Awardees' responsibilities
  • Work on the topic you chose, under your supervisor's guidance.
  • Meet the deadlines you agree to.
  • Send a monthly progress report.
04Supervisors' role

Responsibility: coaching awardees from the beginning through to publication.

Benefit: standing as second-last or last author — optional, and depending on the topic and the supervisor's decision.

05Eligibility
  • Students at all levels, lecturers and researchers in Social Sciences, and in Business and Economics. PhD candidates are not eligible.
  • English is required: IELTS 5.5 or equivalent, at minimum. A certificate is not required — see the FAQ.
06Positions and timeline

The number of positions differs from intake to intake. Commonly:

  • Social Sciences: 04 positions, individual or group.
  • Business & Economics: 04 positions, individual or group.

The proposed timeline is a first draft of each manuscript within six months. The starting time is negotiable.

About team

A program that grew one cohort at a time.

The Seed Publications Program — formerly Academic Writing for Publications, then Seeding Publications Program — is an ongoing, non-profit initiative designed by Dr An Nguyen to give academic support to younger Vietnamese scholars around the globe.

Participants include undergraduate students, master's students and young lecturers and researchers, where "young" is defined by career years rather than age. From K4 onwards, PhD candidates are no longer eligible. What everyone shares is the same starting point: an interest in writing an academic paper, and no clear idea where to begin.

Dr An Nguyen founded the program in March 2024. In late 2024, Dr Dan Tong and Dr Phu Nguyen joined as supervisors and brought their own fields with them, which is how the program came to run parallel tracks in Social Sciences, Business and Economics, and Computer Sciences.

FAQ

Questions people ask before applying.

If yours isn't here, email Dr An Nguyen — questions are genuinely welcome.

I do not have an English certificate. Am I eligible?

Yes. You can apply as long as you believe your English is at least IELTS 5.5 or equivalent.

Do I need a full proposal with my application?

No. You only need the initial idea or topic you would like to work on, plus your updated CV.

I am an undergraduate student. Am I eligible?

Yes, you can apply if you meet the English requirement.

I have papers in domestic journals. Am I eligible?

Yes. The program supports you in publishing in international journals, and you are equally welcome to apply if you have no publications of that kind yet.

Will I pay any hidden fee?

No — the program is free. The only fee you might face is an article processing charge, if you decide to submit to an open-access journal. We don't encourage that, because it is expensive.
